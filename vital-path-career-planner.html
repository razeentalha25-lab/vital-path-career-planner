Resources worth knowing about

Organizations, programs, and accreditation checks that come up again and again in healthcare career planning.

Get experience now

Programs built for students still in high school.

  • HOSA – Future Health Professionals: a national student organization with local chapters, competitions, and healthcare-career events.
  • Hospital volunteer ("candy striper") programs: most hospitals and clinics take teen volunteers 14+; ask your local hospital's volunteer services office.
  • CNA certification courses: often open to 16–18 year-olds through a community college or career-tech center — the fastest real clinical credential you can earn in high school.

Check before you apply

Accreditation is the difference between a credential that transfers and one that doesn't.

  • Nursing programs: look for CCNE or ACEN accreditation.
  • Allied health (rad tech, surgical tech, respiratory, etc.): look for CAAHEP accreditation.
  • Dental hygiene/dentistry: look for CODA accreditation.
  • Pharmacy: look for ACPE accreditation.

Paying for it

Healthcare has more built-in financial support than most fields.

  • NHSC Scholarships & Loan Repayment: federal programs that cover tuition or repay loans in exchange for working in an underserved area after graduating.
  • Hospital tuition assistance: many hospital systems pay for CNA-to-RN or RN-to-BSN progression for their own employees.
  • Community college transfer paths: starting at a two-year school and transferring is often the lowest-debt route into a four-year clinical degree.
